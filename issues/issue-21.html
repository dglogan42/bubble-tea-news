Breaking · Puriri Lane

Someone's Been Leaving Perfectly Ripe Lemons In A Basket At The Bus Stop, Take-One Sign Included

Case definition: a small woven basket, appearing most mornings at the Puriri Lane bus stop, containing between six and ten lemons and a handwritten "take one" sign in the same handwriting each time. Eleven mornings running as of Tuesday. Nobody has claimed responsibility. Nobody has taken more than one lemon, either, which the street finds almost more remarkable than the basket itself.

The council now sends a representative to inspect recycling bins for correct sorting, unannounced, without appointment. I recognize this instrument precisely. Surveillance dressed as civic virtue is still surveillance, and I say that as a man who once found himself on the wrong side of exactly this kind of unannounced inspection, though the stakes, admittedly, were somewhat different.

Technology · Reality Distortion Field

The Lid That Remembers Your Name

This week, a bubble tea shop three blocks from here started printing the customer's name directly onto the cup lid instead of a sticker. That is the plain fact. Everything about how you receive a drink just changed forever. For decades, humanity has been trapped by stickers that peel wrong. Today, that ends. This isn't a lid. It's a statement about what's possible when you stop asking permission to be permanent. Insanely great.

Steve Jobs

Bubble Tea Trivia · Did You Know?

Wintermelon Tea Isn't Actually A Winter Melon

Despite the name, wintermelon tea isn't made from a "winter" melon at all. It's a variety of gourd, also called ash gourd, that's actually harvested in the warmer months.

Markets · The Pearl Index

The December Slowdown, Modelled Before It Happens

Fieldwork window: the two weeks leading into December across five counters, cross-referenced against the same window in each of the last two years I have data for. Method: comparing weekday foot traffic against the November average. Finding: a consistent 8 to 11% dip begins the first week of December every year I've checked (n=690 combined, ±3.2% MOE), presumably as everyone's attention shifts to actual holiday planning. Honest limitation: two years of comparison data is a pattern, not a law, but it's held both times I've had the chance to check it. I'll know for certain by the second week.

Priya Anand, The Pearl Index

Domestic Desk

The Gutter Cleared Before The First Real Rain

Sebastian

The gutters came clear this year two days ahead of the season's first proper downpour, no last-minute scramble up the ladder in the wrong weather, no overflow down the side wall Sebastian has patched more times than he can accurately recall. He stood at the base of the ladder afterward longer than putting it away required. Some years the timing works out. He has learned not to expect it, which is, he supposes, the only way it keeps managing to surprise him.

How Do I Stop Overcommitting Every December?

"Every December I say yes to everything and burn out by New Year's. How do I stop?" Ye can't chart a course by every light on the shore, lad, only the ones worth the detour. Pick your ports before the season starts, not after ye're already three harbours deep and exhausted. Fair winds.

In Memoriam

The Corner Table's Reserved Sign, Finally Removed

Skeletor

The corner table's permanently-reserved sign, held there for a regular who stopped coming in sometime around winter, was quietly removed this week and the table returned to general seating. I confess nothing about how long I personally kept that reservation standing past any reasonable justification. Mourn the sign if sentiment moves you toward laminated cardstock. I remain, as ever, unsentimental about seating charts, which is precisely why I noticed how long it took me to let this one go.
